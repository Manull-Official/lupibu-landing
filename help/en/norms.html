My baby sleeps less (or more) than the norm — is that bad?

3 min read

In short: Almost never. "Norms" are population medians, not targets. If your baby is active, eating, and growing — their norm may differ from the table.

Seeing "12 hours of sleep a day" in a recommendation while yours sleeps 10 is stressful. Here's what actually matters.

What "norm" means

It's a statistical median — half of babies sleep more, half less. Pediatric sleep research gives a wide range (e.g. 0–3 months: 14–17 hours). If your baby is inside the range, you're fine.

When to pay attention

  • Persistent lethargy or hyperarousal
  • Weight not tracking
  • Waking every 30–60 minutes for more than two weeks straight
  • Clear physical symptoms (cough, vomiting, rash)

In those cases — your pediatrician, not an app.

What Lupibu shows

The app shows your data and the age-appropriate range, not a diagnosis. 10.5 hours instead of 12 may simply be who your baby is.

Trust your baby more than the chart.
